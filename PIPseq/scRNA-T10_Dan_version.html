Review the process for rotating the vortex head from horizontal to vertical tube position. The process should be completed in less than 30 seconds.
Dry Bath Operation
The protocol requires a dry bath with 0.5 ml tube block. Make sure that the appropriate block is installed.
The dry bath steps specified in this protocol are specific to the PIPseq dry bath sold as part of the Illumina Single Cell Prep Starter Equipment Kit. If you are using a user-supplied Eppendorf dry bath, you must manually program the lysis and reverse transcription protocols. Refer to Eppendorf's documentation for more detailed information on how to operate their products.
Dry Bath Control
  • Lid temperature is set manually and is independent from the dry bath programs. Understand how to configure lid mode and temperature before starting the protocol.
    • The dry bath interface displays LidOn if the lid temperature control is not active. Select LidOn to activate the lid temperature control. When the lid temperature control is active, the interface displays LidOff.
    • For nuclei lysis and reverse transcription, use the specific temperature control mode to set the lid temperature to 105°C. When set to this mode, the lid temperature box shows the set value (for example, 105.0).
    • For cell lysis, use the offset temperature control mode to set the lid temperature to 5°C higher than the heat block. When set to this mode, the lid temperature box shows the temperature offset (for example +5.0).
    • To change the lid mode, select Edit, and then select LidMode to toggle between 105°C and +5.0, and then select Save/Return.
  • The lid temperature only climbs to 105°C during incubation temperatures of 35°C or higher.
  • The dry bath is preprogrammed with nuclei lysis, cell lysis, and reverse transcription protocols.
  • Preheat and hold settings are depicted as 00:00.
Dry Bath Programs
The following protocols are preprogrammed on the dry bath:
Program A (Cell Lysis): Preheat 25°C · 25°C for 15 minutes · 37°C for 45 minutes · 25°C for 10 minutes · Hold at 20°C
Program B (Nuclei Lysis): Preheat 66°C · 66°C for 45 minutes · 25°C for 10 minutes · Hold at 20°C
Program C (Reverse Transcription): Preheat 25°C · 25°C for 30 minutes · 42°C for 90 minutes · 85°C for 10 minutes · Hold at 4°C

Capture and Lysis

⏱ 1 hr 40 min ⏸ Safe stopping point
▾
Safe stopping point — If you are stopping, leave the tube on the dry bath at 20°C for up to 96 hours. Do not refrigerate or freeze samples.
Reagents — storage & preparation
ReagentSourceKit box / supplierStore atPreparation to use
PIP — T10 PIPs Kit Capture · Box 4 -85 to -65°C Thaw one tube per sample on ice (stable on ice up to 5 h); centrifuge 5 s, keep on ice.
CLB3 — Chemical Lysis Buffer 3 Kit Capture · Box 1 15 to 30°C Use at RT. If crystals are seen, equilibrate on the 25°C dry bath, vortex 10 s, then centrifuge briefly.
PAR — Partitioning Reagent Kit Capture · Box 1 15 to 30°C Use at room temperature.
RNI — RNase Inhibitor (40 U/µl) Kit Capture · Box 3 -25 to -15°C Keep on ice. Use Illumina-supplied RNI for capture & lysis.
Procedure — T10 (verbatim)
Consumables
  • CLB3 (Chemical Lysis Buffer 3)
  • PAR (Partitioning Reagent)
  • RNI (RNase Inhibitor)
  • T10 PIP
  • 0.5 ml Safe-Lock PCR Clean tubes (emulsion safe)
  • 200 µl standard bore, low retention, pipette tips
  • 200 µl wide bore, low retention, pipette tips
  • 1 ml syringe
  • G22 blunt bottom syringe needle
Preparation
  1. Prepare the following consumables:
    • CLB3—If crystals are observed, place on the provided dry bath set to 25°C to equilibrate the tubes to room temperature. Vortex the tubes for 10 seconds, and then centrifuge briefly.
    • T10 PIP—Centrifuge for 5 seconds. Keep on ice.
  2. Label one 0.5 ml Safe-Lock PCR Clean tube per reaction CLB3.
  3. Confirm the vortexer program is set to 3000 rpm for 2 minutes.
  4. Depending on your sample type, select one of the following options to set the lid temperature:
    • For nuclei lysis, select Prog.B, and then manually set the lid temperature to 105°C.
    • For cell lysis, select Prog.A, and then manually set the lid temperature to +5.0.
  5. Select Run, and then select Lid On to preheat the dry bath to the hold temperature for the applicable lysis program.(Dan note: Make sure the program displays "LidOFF", which means the lid temperature is on and set to be +5.)
    The dry bath steps are specific to the PIPseq dry bath sold with the Illumina Single Cell Prep Starter Equipment Kit. With a user-supplied Eppendorf dry bath, manually program the protocols.
🧫 Yeast cells / yeast-containing cells only — suggested stepPlace the vortexer in a 4°C fridge to pre-incubate and prepare for procedure steps 7 to 9.
Procedure
  1. Using a wide-bore 200 µl pipette tip, slowly pipette the cell or nuclei suspension 10 times to mix.
🧫 Yeast cells / yeast-containing cells only — added stepAdd desired amount of zymolyase (2 – 4 µL ideally) directly into the PIPs layer in each PIP tube.
  1. Add 5 µl cell or nuclei suspension at 3,400 cells/µl (17,000 total) directly into the PIPs layer in each PIP tube.
  2. Add 40 units RNase Inhibitor directly into the PIPs layer.
    Dispense slowly to avoid forming bubbles.
  3. Using a standard-bore 200 µl pipette tip, slowly pipette 10 times at the 75 µl stroke to mix.
    When removing the pipette, dispense along the side wall of the tube to avoid excessive foam.
    Generating excessive foam requires additional mixing steps that can cause stress on the cells and lead to poor assay results.
    To mix multiple PIP tubes at once, use a pipette tip on every other channel of a 200 µl multichannel pipette in an alternating configuration.
  4. If excessive foam forms, centrifuge briefly and then slowly pipette 10 times at the 75 µl stroke.
  5. Using a 1000 µl pipette tip, add 320 µl PAR down the side wall of the PIP tube.
  6. Tightly cap tubes and place in the vortex adapter head in the horizontal tube position. Ensure the tubes are fully inserted into the adapter.
  7. Vortex at 3000 rpm and manually stop the program after 15 seconds.
  8. Rotate the vortex adapter into the vertical tube position, and then vortex at 3000 rpm for 2 minutes.
  9. Place tubes in the 0.5 ml side of the combination tube stand and wait until the emulsion separates into two distinct layers (~30 seconds).
    The top layer contains PIPs and cells. The bottom layer contains PAR.
🧫 Yeast cells / yeast-containing cells only — added stepIncubate at room temperature for 30 min for Zymolyase lytic activity.
  1. Using a G22 blunt bottom syringe needle on a 1 ml syringe, remove and discard the PAR waste (bottom phase) as follows.
    1. Slowly lower the syringe tip through the PIPs emulsion (figure A) to the bottom of the tube.
    2. Wait 5 seconds.
    3. Aspirate the bottom phase until the bottom of the PIPs emulsion is even with the B stand mark (figure B). Be careful not to aspirate any emulsion.
    4. While removing the tip, wipe it on the inside of the tube.
    Do not place the PIP tubes on ice for the remainder of the Capture and Lysis steps to avoid precipitation of the lysis reagents.
  2. Combine the following volumes per reaction in the CLB3 tube to prepare the Chemical Lysis Emulsion:
    • CLB3 (90 µl)
    • PAR (270 µl)
    Do not prepare the Chemical Lysis Emulsion in bulk. Prepare each sample aliquot individually.
  3. Vortex the Chemical Lysis Emulsion for 10 seconds, and then use a 1000 µl low retention tip to immediately transfer the entire volume of one CLB3 tube to each PIP tube.
    Mixing CLB3 with PAR before transferring is critical to form the emulsion.
  4. Invert 10–15 times to mix.
  5. Place on the preheated dry bath. Select Skip, and then select Yes to run the applicable lysis program.
    If you are immediately proceeding to Isolate mRNA, prepare reagents while the lysis program is running.
Program A (Cell Lysis): Preheat 25°C · 25°C 15 min · 37°C 45 min · 25°C 10 min · Hold 20°C
Program B (Nuclei Lysis): Preheat 66°C · 66°C 45 min · 25°C 10 min · Hold 20°C
Tips
💡 TipIf zymolyase is added, make sure to add it somewhere in the PIPs layer away from the yeast to minimize yeast cell-wall lysis before forming emulsions.
💡 TipCentrifuge the tube containing PIPs briefly using the minifuge if there are small bubbles observed. Eliminating bubbles before mixing will help produce less foam.
My notes
Common optimization levers: Vortex settings are fixed: 15 s horizontal, then 3000 rpm × 2 min vertical. Mixing CLB3 with PAR immediately before transfer is critical to form the emulsion. Avoid foam when adding cells/RNI. Do not ice the PIP tubes after de-partitioning — it precipitates the lysis reagents.

Isolate mRNA

⏱ Break emulsions 15 min · Wash PIPs 20 min
▾
Reagents — storage & preparation
ReagentSourceKit box / supplierStore atPreparation to use
BBF — Breaking Buffer Kit Capture · Box 2 2 to 8°C Let stand 30 min to bring to room temperature.
DPR — Departitioning Reagent Kit Capture · Box 1 15 to 30°C Use at room temperature.
PWB1 — Wash Buffer Kit Capture · Box 2 2 to 8°C Keep on ice. Dilute to 1X or 0.5X with nuclease-free water as directed.
Procedure — T10 (verbatim)
Consumables
  • BBF (Breaking Buffer)
  • DPR (Departitioning Reagent)
  • PWB1 (Wash Buffer)
  • 0.5 ml & 1.5 ml Safe-Lock PCR Clean tubes (emulsion safe)
  • 1 ml syringe · G22 blunt bottom syringe tip
  • Low-retention pipette tips
Preparation
  1. Prepare PWB1 as follows.
    1. For each sample, label a 1.5 ml tube Wash 1.
    2. Aliquot 1 ml PWB1 to each tube.
    3. Place the Wash 1 tubes on ice.
Procedure — Break Emulsions
  1. Remove PIP tubes from the dry bath and place into the tube stand.
    If condensation appears on the tubes, invert the sample 3 times and let the emulsion settle (~1 minute). Do not centrifuge the emulsion.
  2. Using a G22 blunt bottom syringe needle on a 1 ml syringe, remove and discard the remaining PAR as follows.
    1. Slowly lower the syringe tip through the emulsion (figure A) to the bottom of the tube.
    2. Wait 5 seconds.
    3. Aspirate the bottom phase until the top of the emulsion is below the A marker on the stand (figure B).
      Do not discard any of the PIP layer.
    4. While removing the tip, wipe it on the inside of the tube.
    5. Save the syringe and needle from this step for future steps. If you are processing multiple samples, label the syringe with the sample name.
    Remove as much of the bottom phase as possible. To minimize sample loss, follow best practices for working with PIPs.
  3. Add 300 µl BBF (clear) to each sample, along the side wall of the tube.
  4. Add 100 µl DPR (pink) to each sample, along the side wall of the tube.
  5. Securely close the tube, then fully invert the tube 10–20 times to break the emulsion.
  6. Centrifuge for 10 seconds.
  7. Ensure that the emulsion is completely broken by visually confirming a distinct interface between the pink bottom phase and the upper homogeneous aqueous layer containing the PIPs.
  8. Check for any inhomogeneity in the opaque upper phase, which is a sign of an unbroken emulsion.
  9. If an unbroken emulsion is observed, add 10 µl additional DPR and repeat steps 5 and 6.
  10. If the unbroken emulsion still remains, add 50 µl additional DPR and repeat steps 5 and 6.
  11. Using the syringe, remove all of the pink-colored bottom phase. If a red droplet is observed, remove it with the pink-colored bottom phase.
  12. Centrifuge for 10 seconds.
  13. Using the syringe, remove residual pink-colored bottom phase.
    It is critical to ensure all of the pink bottom phase is removed. It is acceptable to remove a small amount of the clear aqueous to ensure complete removal. If there are unbroken emulsions or pink waste remaining, it can inhibit reverse transcription and cause lower cDNA yield and fragment sizes.
  14. Place the tubes on ice, and then proceed immediately to the next step.
    Do not allow the PIP tubes to freeze. This negatively impacts assay performance.
Wash PIPs
  1. Using a 200 µl low-retention tip, slowly transfer the PIPs from the PIP tube to the chilled Wash 1 tube.
    When transferring PIPs, ensure no PIPs are left in the pipette tip by mixing up and down at least 3 times in the Wash 1 tube.
  2. Briefly centrifuge the PIP tube to collect remaining PIPs.
  3. Transfer all remaining PIPs from the PIP tube to the corresponding Wash 1 tube.
  4. Place the Wash 1 tubes into the stand.
  5. Vortex mix by holding the stand horizontally on a flat-head vortex mixer for ~3 seconds.
  6. Centrifuge for 1 minute.
  7. Without disturbing the pellet, remove supernatant to the 3 volume mark on the stand.
    The pellet is translucent at this step. Removing excess volume can result in loss of PIPs.
  8. Perform an additional wash as follows.
    1. Add 1200 µl 1X PWB1 to each tube.
    2. Vortex mix by holding the stand horizontally on a flat-head vortex mixer for ~3 seconds.
    3. Centrifuge the tubes for 1 minute, and then return tubes to the stand.
    4. Without disturbing the pellet, remove supernatant to the 3 volume mark on the stand.
  9. Repeat steps 8 a–d to wash PIPs a third time. Use 1000 µl 1X PWB1.
  10. Repeat steps 8 a–d to wash PIPs a fourth time. Use 1000 µl 1X PWB1.
My notes
Common optimization levers: Complete removal of the pink bottom phase is critical — residual pink or unbroken emulsion inhibits reverse transcription and lowers cDNA yield and fragment size. Do not let the PIP tubes freeze. When washing, remove supernatant only to the 3-volume mark to avoid PIP loss (the pellet is translucent).

Normalize PIP Volume

⏱ 10 min
▾
Reagents — storage & preparation
No reagents for this step — plasticware only (0.5 ml Safe-Lock PCR Clean tubes).
Procedure — T10 (verbatim)
Consumables
  • 0.5 ml Safe-Lock PCR Clean tubes (emulsion safe)
Preparation
  1. Use a permanent marker to mark the 0.1 ml volume engraving on the tube.
Procedure
  1. Transfer the entire volume (~200 µl) of each PIP mixture to a marked 0.5 ml Safe-Lock PCR Clean tube.
  2. Centrifuge for 1 minute.
    Process two reactions at a time during centrifuge and supernatant removal steps to ensure optimal PIP pelleting.
  3. Keeping the tube at an angle, use a 200 µl pipette to immediately remove the supernatant down to the 0.1 ml mark on the tube.
    Do not disrupt packed PIPs.
  4. Place tubes on ice and proceed immediately to cDNA synthesis.
My notes
Common optimization levers: Removing supernatant down to the 0.1 ml mark without disrupting packed PIPs normalizes sample concentration for cDNA synthesis. Process two reactions at a time during the centrifuge and supernatant-removal steps for optimal, consistent pelleting.

Wash PIPs for cDNA Amplification

⏱ 30 min
▾
Reagents — storage & preparation
ReagentSourceKit box / supplierStore atPreparation to use
PWB1 — Wash Buffer Kit Capture · Box 2 2 to 8°C Keep on ice. Dilute to 1X or 0.5X with nuclease-free water as directed.
NFW — Nuclease-free water Customer Thermo #AM9937 or general lab Room temp Use as directed (dilutions, resuspension).
Procedure — T10 (verbatim)
Consumables
  • PWB1 (Wash Buffer)
  • Nuclease-free water (customer provided)
Procedure
  1. Combine the following volumes to prepare 0.5X PWB1. Multiply each volume by the number of samples.
    • PWB1 (625 µl)
    • Nuclease-free water (625 µl)
    These volumes produce 1250 µl 0.5X PWB1 per sample. (Dan note:Do not discard after use if you have extra, since you will need to use the same buffer for the next step.)
  2. Centrifuge the PIP tubes for 1 minute.
  3. Without disturbing the pellet, remove and discard 130 µl supernatant.
  4. Wash PIPs as follows.
    1. Add 450 µl 0.5X PWB1.
    2. Vortex for 5 seconds.
    3. Centrifuge for 1 minute.
    4. Without disturbing the pellet, remove and discard supernatant to the A mark on the tube holder.
  5. Repeat steps 4 a–d to wash PIPs a second time. Use 350 µl 0.5X PWB1.
  6. Repeat steps 4 a–d to wash PIPs a third time. Use 350 µl 0.5X PWB1.
    Process two samples at a time to ensure optimal PIP pelleting and to avoid PIP loss.
  7. Centrifuge for 1 minute.

  1. Resuspend magnetic beads by vortexing well.
  2. For 100 µl reaction volumes, add 80 µl clean up beads. If needed, adjust the bead volume for a 0.8X ratio of beads.

  3. Pipette 15 times with the pipette set to 170 µl to mix.

This step dilutes libraries to the starting concentration for your sequencing system and is the first step in a serial dilution. After diluting to the starting concentration, libraries are ready to be denatured and diluted to the final loading concentration.
Procedure
